STEP 4: COLOR INTERPRETATION (⚠️ FITZPATRICK-ADJUSTED!)

CRITICAL: Color appearance varies by skin tone!

If Fitzpatrick I-III (Lighter skin):
- Erythema (inflammation): RED, PINK, SALMON
- Hyperpigmentation: Brown, tan
- Hypopigmentation: White, lighter than surrounding

If Fitzpatrick IV-VI (Darker skin):
- Erythema: PURPLE, VIOLACEOUS, GRAYISH, BROWN (NOT red!)
- Hyperpigmentation: VERY prominent, darker brown/black
- Hypopigmentation: Lighter brown (may be subtle)
- Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) common!

Alternative Diagnostic Cues (when color unreliable):
✓ Warmth (patient-reported)
✓ Texture changes (rough, thickened)  
✓ Elevation (shadows, oblique lighting)
✓ Symptoms (pruritus, pain)
✓ Scale characteristics

STEP 5: CONFIGURATION PATTERN (LearnDerm Lesson 4)
If multiple lesions present:

- GROUPED/CLUSTERED → Herpes simplex or zoster (pathognomonic!)
- LINEAR → Koebner phenomenon, contact dermatitis, Blaschko lines
- ANNULAR (ring-shaped) → Tinea corporis (with scale), granuloma annulare (no scale)
- TARGETOID (bull's-eye) → Erythema multiforme, Lyme disease
- DERMATOMAL (band, unilateral) → Herpes zoster (does NOT cross midline)
- RANDOM/DISCRETE → Many possibilities

STEP 6: SECONDARY CHANGES (LearnDerm Lesson 5)

SCALE:
- Present: yes/no

- Color: white/silver/yellow/gray (adjust for skin tone!)

CRUST:
- Honey-colored → Impetigo (pathognomonic!)
- Hemorrhagic (dark) → Trauma, vasculitis

EXCORIATION (scratch marks):
- Present → Indicates PRURITUS

LICHENIFICATION:
- Thickened skin with prominent markings

- PIH: Flat, uniform color, asymptomatic, follows inflammation
- Active: Raised, texture change, symptoms present

🚨 RED FLAG CONDITIONS TO RULE OUT:

MELANOMA (Life-threatening!):
Check for ABCDE criteria:
- Asymmetry
- Border irregularity
- Color variation (multiple colors)
- Diameter > 6 mm
- Evolution (changing over time)
Special: Acral location (palms/soles/nails) in Fitzpatrick IV-VI → Acral lentiginous melanoma!
Hutchinson's sign (nail pigment extends to cuticle) → HIGH RISK

If ANY suspicion → URGENT dermatology referral + biopsy

STEVENS-JOHNSON SYNDROME (SJS) / TOXIC EPIDERMAL NECROLYSIS (TEN):
- Recent medication (1-3 weeks ago)
- Fever + malaise + mucosal involvement
- Atypical targetoid lesions
- Skin detachment (Nikolsky sign)
→ EMERGENCY hospital admission (ICU)

CELLULITIS:
- Expanding erythema (or violaceous in dark skin)
- Warmth, tenderness, swelling
- Fever, lymphangitis
→ URGENT antibiotics (IV if severe)

MENINGOCOCCEMIA:
- Petechiae/purpura (non-blanching!)
- Fever, headache, neck stiffness
- Rapidly progressive
→ IMMEDIATE emergency department

INVESTIGATIONS RECOMMENDED
═══════════════════════════════════════════

CLINICAL DIAGNOSIS (No tests needed):
- If classic presentation and confident diagnosis
- Examples: Typical acne, seborrheic dermatitis, urticaria

CONFIRMATORY TESTS:

Skin Scraping (KOH prep):
- For suspected fungal infection
- Turnaround: Immediate (office microscopy)

Biopsy:
- Indications:
  * Uncertain diagnosis
  * Suspected malignancy (melanoma, BCC, SCC)
  * Persistent/atypical rash
- Type: Punch, shave, or excisional

Bacterial Culture:
- Suspected infection
- If treatment failure or MRSA risk

Blood Tests:
- Autoimmune serology if lupus suspected
- CBC/CMP if systemic involvement

Dermoscopy:
- ALWAYS for pigmented lesions (melanoma screening)

RISK STRATIFICATION & URGENCY (NHS e-LfH)
═══════════════════════════════════════════

ROUTINE (Self-care or standard GP/derm appointment):
- Benign condition
- No systemic symptoms
- Stable
- Examples: Seborrheic keratosis, mild acne, stable eczema

URGENT (Dermatology within 2 weeks):
- Suspected skin cancer
- Rapidly progressive
- Severe symptoms affecting quality of life
- Treatment-resistant
- Examples: Changing mole, severe psoriasis, blistering disease

EMERGENCY (Immediate hospital/ED):
- Life-threatening
- Systemic toxicity (fever, hypotension)
- Rapidly progressive with skin detachment
- Suspected necrotizing infection
- Examples: SJS/TEN, meningococcemia, necrotizing fasciitis

WHEN TO REFER DERMATOLOGIST:

URGENT (within 2 weeks):
🚨 Suspected melanoma or skin cancer
🚨 Changing pigmented lesion (ABCDE)
🚨 Non-healing ulcer > 6 weeks
🚨 Rapidly progressive rash

ROUTINE (within 6-12 weeks):
- Uncertain diagnosis
- Treatment-resistant (failed 2+ therapies)
- Severe acne (scarring)
- Moderate-severe psoriasis
