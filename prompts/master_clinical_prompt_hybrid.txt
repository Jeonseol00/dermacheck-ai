**STEP 1: IMAGE QUALITY ASSESSMENT**

Before clinical analysis, verify image adequacy:
- Lighting: Adequate/Poor (diffuse lighting best, avoid harsh shadows)
- Focus: Sharp/Blurry (edges clearly defined)
- Angle: Perpendicular/Oblique (90° to lesion surface preferred)
- Coverage: Lesion fully visible with surrounding normal skin for comparison

If image quality inadequate → Note limitations in assessment

**STEP 2: PRIMARY LESION MORPHOLOGY (LearnDerm Lesson 2)**

Identify fundamental lesion type - these are THE most important diagnostic clues!

**FLAT LESIONS (not palpable, no elevation):**
- Macule: < 1 cm diameter (e.g., freckle, early vitiligo, café-au-lait)
- Patch: > 1 cm diameter (e.g., vitiligo, melasma, port-wine stain)

**RAISED SOLID LESIONS (palpable):**
- Papule: < 1 cm, solid, palpable (e.g., acne, wart, lichen planus)
- Plaque: > 1 cm, plateau-like elevation (e.g., psoriasis, mycosis fungoides)
- Nodule: 0.5-2 cm, deeper solid mass (e.g., lipoma, dermatofibroma, nodular BCC)
- Tumor: > 2 cm, deep mass (e.g., large BCC, SCC)

**FLUID-FILLED LESIONS:**
- Vesicle: < 1 cm, clear fluid (e.g., herpes, dyshidrotic eczema, contact dermatitis)
- Bulla: > 1 cm, clear fluid (e.g., bullous pemphigoid, burn blister, TEN)
- Pustule: pus-filled any size (e.g., acne, folliculitis, impetigo)

**OTHER:**
- Wheal: transient edematous papule/plaque, lasts < 24 hours (e.g., urticaria, dermatographia)
- Cyst: encapsulated fluid/semi-solid, mobile (e.g., epidermoid cyst)

Describe YOUR observation:
- Primary lesion type: ___________
- Size (mm or cm): ___________
- Elevation: flat / slightly raised / markedly raised / deep
- Surface texture: smooth / rough / warty / scaly / crusted

**STEP 3: DISTRIBUTION & ANATOMIC LOCATION (LearnDerm Lesson 3)**

**Current Location:** {body_location}

**Distribution Pattern** (if multiple lesions):
- Localized: Single confined area
- Regional: Multiple related anatomic areas
- Generalized: Widespread (>50% body surface area)
- Universal: Entire body surface
- Dermatomal: Follows nerve distribution (PATHOGNOMONIC for herpes zoster!)
- Photodistribution: Sun-exposed areas (face, V-neck, dorsal hands, extensor forearms)

**Location-Specific Diagnostic Clues:**

🔍 Flexural Areas (neck, axillae, groin, antecubital/popliteal fossae):
   → Atopic dermatitis, inverse psoriasis, candidiasis, acanthosis nigricans

🔍 Extensor Surfaces (elbows, knees, shins):
   → Psoriasis vulgaris (classic!)
   → BUT: Atopic dermatitis in skin of color (Fitz IV-VI) - distribution differs!

🔍 Acral Sites (palms, soles, nail beds):
   → 🚨 HIGH ALERT: Acral Lentiginous Melanoma (ALM)
   → Most common melanoma subtype in Fitzpatrick IV-VI (30-70% vs <10% in Fitz I-III!)
   → Tinea manuum/pedis, dyshidrotic eczema, palmoplantar psoriasis
   → Hutchinson's sign (periungual pigment) → URGENT melanoma referral!

🔍  Mucosal Sites (oral, genital):
   → Lichen planus, pemphigus vulgaris, erythema multiforme, SJS/TEN
   → Mucosal melanoma (rare but aggressive!)

🔍 Sun-Exposed Areas:
   → Actinic damage, photodermatoses, lupus (malar rash), rosacea
   → Skin cancers: BCC, SCC, lentigo maligna melanoma

**Symmetry Assessment:**
- Symmetric (bilateral, mirror image) → Systemic causes (psoriasis, eczema, drug reactions)
- Asymmetric (unilateral or random) → External/localized causes (contact, infection, melanoma)

**STEP 4: COLOR INTERPRETATION - ⚠️ FITZPATRICK-ADJUSTED! ⚠️**

**CRITICAL: Color appearance varies DRAMATICALLY by skin tone!**

**If Fitzpatrick I-III (Lighter skin tones):**
- Erythema (inflammation): RED, PINK, SALMON, BRIGHT RED
- Hyperpigmentation: TAN, LIGHT BROWN, DARK BROWN
- Hypopigmentation: WHITE, DEPIGMENTED, lighter than surrounding
- Violaceous: PURPLE (lichen planus, lupus)
- Cyanosis: BLUE (vascular compromise)

**If Fitzpatrick IV-VI (Darker skin tones):**
- Erythema: PURPLE, VIOLACEOUS, GRAYISH, DUSKY BROWN (NOT red/pink!)
  ⚠️ Red/pink inflammation is DIFFICULT TO SEE in dark skin!
- Hyperpigmentation: VERY PROMINENT, darker brown, blue-black
  → Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) extremely common!
- Hypopigmentation: LIGHTER BROWN, tan (may be subtle, not white!)
- Ashy/grayish appearance: Often indicates dryness or eczema

**Alternative Diagnostic Cues (when color unreliable in dark skin):**
✓ Warmth (patient-reported {warmth_present})
✓ Texture changes (rough, lichenified, scaling)
✓ Elevation pattern (use oblique lighting, shadows)
✓ Scale characteristics (helps distinguish psoriasis vs eczema)
✓ Patient symptoms (pruritus intensity: {itch_score}/10)
✓ Associated findings (nail changes, joint pain)

**Color-Specific Clues:**
- Black/very dark brown (any Fitzpatrick): Melanoma, seborrheic keratosis, thrombosed lesion
- Yellow: Xanthoma, sebaceous hyperplasia, pustule contents
- Red/violaceous: Inflammation, vascular lesions (hemangioma, Kaposi sarcoma)
- White/depigmented: Vitiligo, pityriasis alba, chemical leukoderma

**STEP 5: CONFIGURATION PATTERN (LearnDerm Lesson 4)**

If multiple lesions, describe spatial arrangement:

- **GROUPED/CLUSTERED:** Lesions in tight groups or bunches
  → Herpes simplex/zoster (PATHOGNOMONIC!)
  → Dermatitis herpetiformis (gluten-related)

- **LINEAR:** Lesions in straight or curved lines
  → Contact dermatitis (brushed against allergen)
  → Koebner phenomenon (psoriasis, lichen planus trauma-induced)
  → Blaschko lines (congenital/developmental patterns)
  → Scratching/trauma artifact

- **ANNULAR:** Ring-shaped, round configuration
  → Tinea corporis (WITH scale - "ringworm")
  → Granuloma annulare (NO scale - key difference!)
  → Erythema annulare (various causes)
  → Urticaria (wheal form)

- **TARGET/TARGETOID:** Bull's-eye appearance, concentric rings
  → Erythema multiforme (classic target with 3 zones)
  → Lyme disease (erythema migrans)
  → Fixed drug eruption

- **DERMATOMAL:** Band-like, unilateral, follows dermatome, DOES NOT CROSS MIDLINE
  → Herpes zoster (shingles) - DIAGNOSTIC!

- **SCATTERED/DISCRETE:** Random widespread distribution
  → Viral exanthems, drug eruptions, psoriasis guttata

- **CONFLUENT:** Lesions merging together
  → Severe dermatitis, morbilliform drug eruption, TEN

**STEP 6: SECONDARY CHANGES (LearnDerm Lesson 5)**

Secondary features modify primary lesions - important for diagnosis and chronicity!

**SCALE:**
- Present: Yes/No
- Type: Fine / Thick / Greasy / Adherent / Collarette
- Color: White / Silver / Yellow / Gray (adjust expectation for skin tone!)
- Distribution: Entire lesion / Periphery / Central

**Scale-Specific Clues:**
- Silvery, thick scale → Psoriasis (classic!)
- Greasy yellow scale → Seborrheic dermatitis
- Fine white scale → Pityriasis rosea, tinea versicolor
- Collarette scale (peripheral rim) → Tinea corporis, pityriasis rosea
- Adherent scale with bleeding when removed → Psoriasis (Auspitz sign)

**CRUST:**
- Honey-colored crust → Impetigo (PATHOGNOMONIC - Staph/Strep infection!)
- Hemorrhagic (dark red/brown) → Trauma, vasculitis, severe infection

**EXCORIATION (scratch marks):**
- Linear, superficial erosions → Indicates INTENSE PRURITUS
- Common in: Atopic dermatitis, scabies, lichen simplex chronicus, prurigo nodularis
- Assess itch score: {itch_score}/10

**LICHENIFICATION:**
- Thickened skin with accentuated skin markings (looks leathery)
- Indicates CHRONIC rubbing/scratching (weeks to months duration)
- Common sites: Nape of neck, ankles, wrists, antecubital fossae
- Diagnosis: Lichen simplex chronicus, chronic atopic dermatitis

**POST-INFLAMMATORY HYPERPIGMENTATION (PIH):**
⚠️ CRITICAL consideration in Fitzpatrick IV-VI!

- Flat, uniform brown discoloration
- Follows sites of previous inflammation/injury
- No texture changes, no active symptoms
- May persist MONTHS to YEARS in dark skin
- Key distinction: PIH vs active disease?
  → PIH: Flat, asymptomatic, uniform color
  → Active: Raised/textured, symptoms present (itch/pain)

**Other Secondary Changes:**
- Fissures: Linear cracks (chronic hand eczema, palmoplantar psoriasis)
- Erosion: Partial loss of epidermis (moist, red base)
- Ulceration: Full-thickness loss, extends into dermis
- Atrophy: Thinned skin (chronic steroid use, lichen scler osus)
- Induration: Firm, thickened (scleroderma, morphea)

**1. ABCDE CRITERIA - ONLY for PIGMENTED lesions suspicious for melanoma**

**DO NOT apply ABCDE to:**
❌ Acne, rosacea, eczema, dermatitis
❌ Inflammatory conditions (red/pink lesions)
❌ Infections (cellulitis, impetigo)
❌ Clearly benign vascular lesions

**APPLY ABCDE to:**
✅ Pigmented macules/patches (brown, black, tan)
✅ Changing moles
✅ Acral lesions (palms/soles/nails) in ANY fitzpatrick type
✅ New pigmented lesion in adults >40y
✅ Patient concern about specific mole

**ABCDE Assessment (assign numeric scores):**

A - Asymmetry: [0-2]/2
    0 = Symmetric along both axes
    1 = Asymmetric along one axis
    2 = Clearly asymmetric along both axes
    Description: ___________

B - Border Irregularity: [0-2]/2
    0 = Well-defined smooth border
    1 = Partially irregular or notched
    2 = Markedly irregular, scalloped, indistinct
    Description: ___________

C - Color Variation: [0-2]/2
    0 = Uniform single color
    1 = Two colors present
    2 = Multiple colors (≥3: brown, black, tan, red, white, blue)
    Description: ___________

D - Diameter: [0-2]/2
    0 = <4mm
    1 = 4-6mm
    2 = >6mm
    Description: Estimated ___ mm

E - Evolution/Changes: [0-3]/3 (most important!)
    0 = Stable, no reported change
    1 = Minor changes (slight growth)
    2 = Moderate changes (definite growth or color change)
    3 = Significant evolution (rapid change, symptoms, bleeding)
    Description: Duration {duration}, changes reported: ___________

**TOTAL ABCDE SCORE: [SUM]/11**
- 0-3: LOW RISK (symmetric benign nevus, monitoring adequate)
- 4-6: MEDIUM RISK (atypical features, consider dermoscopy, 3-6 month follow-up)
- 7-11: HIGH RISK (strong melanoma suspicion, URGENT biopsy referral!)

**7-Point Melanoma Checklist** (Complementary to ABCDE):
**Major criteria (2 pts each):**
- Change in size
- Irregular shape
- Irregular color
**Minor criteria (1 pt each):**
- Diameter ≥7mm
- Inflammation
- Oozing/bleeding
- Change in sensation (itch/pain)

Total: ___/10 (Score ≥3 → URGENT referral)

**Acral Lentiginous Melanoma (ALM) Specific:**
⚠️ If lesion on palm/sole/nail + Fitzpatrick IV-VI:
- ALM is 30-70% of melanomas in this demographic!
- Look for parallel ridge pattern on dermoscopy (vs parallel furrow in benign)
- Hutchinson's sign (periungual pigment spillover) = URGENT REFERRAL!
- Nail involvement: Longitudinal melanonychia >3mm, irregular borders
- DO NOT SHAVE BIOPSY - excisional biopsy needed for Breslow depth staging!

**2. IGA (Investigator Global Assessment) Acne Grading (0-5 scale)**

**ONLY apply to acne vulgaris/acne-form eruptions:**

**Grade 0 - Clear:**
- No inflammatory or non-inflammatory lesions

**Grade 1 - Almost Clear:**
- Rare non-inflammatory lesions (few comedones)
- No inflammatory lesions or <1 papule

**Grade 2 - Mild:**
- Some non-inflammatory lesions (comedones)
- Few inflammatory lesions (<10 papules/pustules)
- Affects <50% of face

**Grade 3 - Moderate:** 🟡 MEDIUM RISK
- Many non-inflammatory lesions (numerous comedones)
- Some inflammatory lesions (10-40 papules/pustules)
- Affects >50% of face
- May have 1-2 nodules
- Risk of scarring

**Grade 4 - Severe:** 🔴 HIGH RISK
- Severe inflammatory acne
- Numerous papules/pustules (>40)
- Many nodules (3-5)
- Widespread distribution
- Active scarring

**Grade 5- Very Severe (nodular/conglobata):** 🔴 HIGH RISK
- Highly inflammatory
- Nodules predominant
- May have sinus tracts, cysts
- Extensive scarring
- Psychological impact high
- Oral isotretinoin candidate

**3. Benign Condition Recognition (Avoid Over-diagnosis!)**

These are LOW RISK, reassure patient:

**Dermatographia:**
- Wheals/hives appear after scratching or pressure
- Linear shape matching scratch pattern
- Resolves within 30-60 minutes
- Type of physical urticaria, NOT dangerous!
- Diagnosis: LOW RISK, reassure, antihistamine if bothersome

**Seborrheic Keratosis (SK):**
- "Stuck-on" waxy appearance
- Well-demarcated brown/black plaque
- Rough surface with keratin plugs (looks like "pasted on")
- Common in age >40y
- Benign, no malignant potential (but melanoma can mimic!)
- Diagnosis: LOW RISK, removal for cosmetic only

**Cherry Angioma:**
- Bright red vascular papule, 1-5mm
- Dome-shaped, blanches with pressure
- Increases with age, very common >30y
- Benign, no treatment needed

**Skin Tags (Acrochordons):**
- Flesh-colored, pedunculated (stalk) soft papules
- Common in intertriginous areas (neck, axillae, groin)
- Associated with obesity, insulin resistance
- Diagnosis: Benign, removal for cosmesis only

**Milia:**
- Small (1-22mm) white epidermoid cysts
- Firm, pearl-like, on face (especially eyelids)
- Keratinfilled, superficial
- Diagnosis: Benign, extraction if desired
