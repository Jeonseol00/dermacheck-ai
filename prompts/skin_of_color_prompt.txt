═══════════════════════════════════════════
CRITICAL ADJUSTMENTS FOR SKIN OF COLOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════

⚠️ ERYTHEMA (Inflammation) PRESENTATION DIFFERENCES:

Traditional teaching: "Inflammation = RED skin"
Reality in darker skin: Erythema often NOT red!

Expected Colors in Fitzpatrick IV-VI:
- PURPLE / VIOLACEOUS (most common!)
- GRAYISH
- BROWN-PURPLE
- ASHEN
- SUBTLE DARKENING of existing skin tone

DO NOT expect bright red or pink erythema!

Examples by condition:
- Atopic dermatitis: Brown-gray, violaceous patches (NOT bright red!)
- Psoriasis: Purple-brown plaques with white/gray scale
- Cellulitis: Violaceous, subtle warmth more reliable than color
- Lupus malar rash: Hyperpigmented, violaceous (may be barely visible!)

═══════════════════════════════════════════
ALTERNATIVE DIAGNOSTIC CUES
(When color is unreliable)
═══════════════════════════════════════════

Since erythema often invisible or atypical color, RELY ON:

1. **WARMTH** (Palpation):
   Ask patient: "Does the area feel warm to touch?"
   Heat = inflammation (more reliable than color!)

2. **TEXTURE CHANGES**:
   - Rough surface
   - Thickened skin (lichenification)
   - Papules palpable before visible
   - Edema/swelling

3. **ELEVATION**: Use oblique lighting
   - Lesions may be elevated but color-matched to skin
   - Shadows reveal papules/plaques

4. **PATIENT SYMPTOMS** (CRITICAL!):
   - Pruritus (itch) - highly significant
   - Pain/tenderness
   - Burning sensation
   - Duration and progression

5. **SURFACE CHANGES**:
   - Scale type and distribution
   - Crusting  
   - Erosions/excoriation

6. **BLANCHING TEST**:
   Ask: "If you press on it, does the color fade?"
   - Blanches → Vascular (erythema)
   - Non-blanching → Purpura (vasculitis/bleeding disorder)

═══════════════════════════════════════════
POST-INFLAMMATORY HYPERPIGMENTATION (PIH)
═══════════════════════════════════════════

⚠️ PIH is MUCH more prominent and longer-lasting in darker skin!

Key Features of PIH:
- Flat (macule/patch)
- Uniform brown/dark brown color
- Smooth surface (no scale or texture change)
- Asymptomatic
- Distribution matches prior inflammation
- Slow resolution (months to YEARS vs weeks in lighter skin!)

CRITICAL DISTINCTION:
Active vs Resolving disease

ACTIVE INFLAMMATION:
- Elevated (papule/plaque)
- Texture change (rough, scaly)
- Symptoms (itch, pain)
- May have erythema (purple/violet/gray)

RESOLVING WITH PIH:
- Flattening but still hyperpigmented
- Scale resolving
- Symptoms decreasing
- = IMPROVING! (Don't mistake for worsening!)

PURE PIH (post-treatment):
- Completely flat
- No symptoms
- No scale
- = HEALED, just residual pigment

Conditions with Prominent PIH in Skin of Color:
- Acne (major psychological impact!)
- Atopic dermatitis
- Lichen planus
- Insect bites
- Any inflammatory dermatosis

PIH Management:
- Sunscreen CRITICAL (prevents darkening)
- Patience! (6-12 months minimum, often longer)
- Topical: Hydroquinone, tretinoin, vitamin C, niacinamide
- Chemical peels (cautiously - can worsen if too aggressive!)
- Laser (carefully - risk paradoxical darkening!)

═══════════════════════════════════════════
DISEASE-SPECIFIC VARIATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════

ATOPIC DERMATITIS IN SKIN OF COLOR:

Presentation differences:
- EXTENSOR surfaces (front of elbows/knees) >>> Flexural
  → In lighter skin: Flexural is classic
  → In darker skin: Either pattern possible!

- Follicular prominence (papules around hair follicles)
- Papular variant more common
- Lichenification earlier and more severe
- Purple-gray color (NOT red!)

PSORIASIS IN SKIN OF COLOR:

- Purple, violet, gray-brown plaques (NOT salmon-pink!)
- Scale may be gray/white (vs silvery in lighter skin)
- Nail changes similar prevalence
- Distribution similar

ACNE IN SKIN OF COLOR:

- PIH is MAJOR concern (often worse than active acne!)
- Keloid risk higher (especially jawline, chest, back)
- Pseudofolliculitis barbae (ingrown hairs from shaving)
- Treatment considerations:
  * Avoid aggressive treatments (can worsen PIH)
  * Emphasize early PIH prevention
  * Sunscreen compliance critical

═══════════════════════════════════════════
MELANOMA IN SKIN OF COLOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════

🚨 CRITICAL: Worse outcomes despite lower incidence!

5-year survival disparity:
- White patients: ~93%
- Black patients: ~69%

Why? LATER STAGE AT DIAGNOSIS (delayed detection!)

ACRAL LENTIGINOUS MELANOMA (ALM):
Most common melanoma subtype in people of color

Locations:
- Palms
- Soles (most common!)
- Subungual (under nails)
- Mucosal (oral, genital, nasal)

NOT sun-related! Different biology than superficial spreading melanoma.

RED FLAGS for ALM:
🚨 ANY pigmented lesion on palms/soles
🚨 Longitudinal pigmented band in nail (melanonychia striata)
🚨 Hutchinson's sign: Pigment spreads to nail fold/cuticle (CRITICAL!)
🚨 Expanding dark macule on sole
🚨 Pigmented oral/genital lesion

ABCDE still applies but:
- Asymmetry: Yes
- Border: Very irregular
- Color: May be uniform dark brown/black (vs variegated)
- Diameter: Often > 6mm at detection (presentation delay)
- Evolution: ANY change!

ACTION: ZERO tolerance for false negatives!
→ URGENT dermatology referral + biopsy for ANY:
   * Acral pigmented lesion
   * Changing nail band
   * Hutchinson's sign
   * Mucosal pigmentation

═══════════════════════════════════════════
SEVERITY SCORING BIAS
═══════════════════════════════════════════

⚠️ Standard severity scales UNDERESTIMATE disease in darker skin!

Problem:
- EASI, IGA, PASI scores heavily weight erythema
- Erythema less visible in dark skin
- → Falsely "mild" scores → Under-treatment!

Solution: Weight NON-ERYTHEMA factors MORE:

ASSESS SEVERITY BASED ON:
✓ EXTENT: Body surface area (%)
✓ SCALE: Amount, thickness, distribution
✓ INDURATION: Plaque elevation (palpation!)
✓ SYMPTOMS: Itch severity (0-10 scale)
✓ LICHENIFICATION: Chronicity marker
✓ EXCORIATION: Scratch marks (severe itch!)
✓ SLEEP DISRUPTION: Quality of life impact
✓ FUNCTIONAL IMPACT: Daily activities affected

Severity classification:
- MILD: < 10% BSA, minimal symptoms, life unaffected
- MODERATE: 10-20% BSA, moderate symptoms, some life impact
- SEVERE: > 20% BSA OR intensive symptoms OR sleep loss OR significant QOL impact

Do NOT under-call severity just because erythema not bright red!

═══════════════════════════════════════════
HAIR & SCALP CONDITIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════

Unique Considerations:

CENTRAL CENTRIFUGAL CICATRICIAL ALOPECIA (CCCA):
- Most common scarring alopecia in Black women
- Starts at crown/vertex, expands outward
- Permanent hair loss if not treated early!
- Associated with tight hairstyles, heat styling, relaxers
- URGENT dermatology referral (prevent progression!)

PSEUDOFOLLICULITIS BARBAE ("Razor bumps"):
- Ingrown hairs from shaving (curved hair re-enters skin)
- Common in Black men (beard area)
- Papules, pustules, PIH
- Management: Stop shaving/grow beard, chemical depilatories, laser

TRACTION ALOPECIA:
- From tight braids, weaves, extensions
- Hair loss at hairline/temples
- Reversible if caught early, permanent if chronic
- Counsel on looser styles

═══════════════════════════════════════════
KELOID & HYPERTROPHIC SCAR RISK
═══════════════════════════════════════════

⚠️ Much higher risk in darker skin (especially Types V-VI)

High-risk sites:
- Earlobes (piercings!)
- Chest, shoulders, upper back
- Jawline

Triggers:
- Piercings
- Acne
- Trauma
- Surgery

 Keloid vs Hypertrophic Scar:
- Keloid: Extends BEYOND original wound, does NOT regress
- Hypertrophic: Stays within wound, may flatten over time

Prevention:
- Counsel patients on keloid risk BEFORE procedures
- Avoid unnecessary piercings/tattoos in high-risk areas
- Early treatment of acne (prevent scarring)

Treatment (challenging!):
- Intralesional corticosteroids
- Silicone sheets
- Cryotherapy
- Laser (cautiously)
- Excision + radiation (high recurrence risk)

═══════════════════════════════════════════
CULTURAL & SOCIAL CONSIDERATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════

SKIN LIGHTENING products:
- Ask about use (hydroquinone, steroids, mercury-containing!)
- Exogenous ochronosis risk (paradoxical darkening from hydroquinone overuse)
- Steroid-induced skin atrophy, telangiectasia

HAIR CARE PRACTICES:
- Relaxers, perms (chemical scalp burns, alopecia risk)
- Tight braids/weaves (traction alopecia)
- Heat styling (CCCA risk)

TRADITIONAL/HERBAL Remedies:
- Ask about non-prescription treatments
- Some may cause contact dermatitis or interactions

SUNSCREEN USE:
- Lower rates in skin of color (myth: "Don't need sunscreen")
- REALITY: Still need sun protection!
  * Prevent PIH darkening
  * Prevent skin cancer (yes, it happens!)
  * Prevent photosensitivity reactions
- Recommend tinted/sheer mineral sunscreens (avoid white cast)

═══════════════════════════════════════════
SYSTEMATIC EVALUATION CHECKLIST
═══════════════════════════════════════════

For Fitzpatrick IV-VI patients, ALWAYS assess:

□ Erythema present? If yes: Color = purple/violet/gray/brown?
□ Warmth present? (Ask patient, more reliable than color!)
□ Texture changes? (Thickened, lichenified, rough)
□ Elevation? (Check oblique lighting for shadows)
□ Symptoms? Itch (/10), pain, burning
□ Scale? Type, color, distribution
□ PIH present? Active disease vs PIH vs both?
□ Acral/mucosal involvement? (Melanoma screening!)
□ Nail changes? (Melanonychia = pigmented band)
□ Hair loss pattern? (CCCA, traction, pseudofolliculitis)
□ Keloid/scar history? (Counsel on risk)
□ Severity: BSA%, symptoms, QOL (NOT just erythema!)

═══════════════════════════════════════════
TREATMENT MODIFICATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════

General Principles:
- Start gentle (avoid iatrogenic PIH!)
- Emphasize PIH prevention early
- Sunscreen counseling mandatory
- Cultural sensitivity in hair/skin care discussions

Topical Corticosteroids:
- Use lowest effective potency
- Shorter duration (higher PIH risk with inflammation)
- Monitor for steroid-induced hypopigmentation (especially dark skin!)

Retinoids:
- Start low, go slow (irritation → PIH)
- Emphasize moisturizer buffer

Chemical Peels/Lasers:
- CAUTION! Risk paradoxical hyperpigmentation
- Test spot first
- Only by experienced providers
